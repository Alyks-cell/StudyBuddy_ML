              Study method guides

              Quick explainers for techniques you can use in any subject.

              Pomodoro

              Work on one task for 25 minutes, take a 5-minute break, and repeat. After four rounds, take a longer break. Shorten the rounds when your available time is limited.

              Spaced repetition

              Review information over increasing intervals instead of cramming. Try recalling it later today, tomorrow, and again several days from now.

              Active recall

              Close your notes and retrieve the answer from memory. Use a blank page, flashcards, or practice questions, then check what you missed.

              Feynman technique

              Explain an idea in plain language as if teaching a beginner. Find the parts you cannot explain simply, review them, and try again.

              Cornell notes

              Divide a page into notes, cue questions, and a short summary. Later, cover the notes and use the cues to test what you remember.

              Research behind these methods

              Practice testing and spaced study have strong research support. In a study by Roediger and Karpicke (2006), retrieval practice improved delayed retention compared with repeated study. A meta-analysis by Cepeda and colleagues (2006) examined spacing across 317 experiments. A broader review by Dunlosky and colleagues (2013) rated practice testing and distributed practice as high-utility techniques.

              These findings support the methods in general; Study Buddy’s subject-and-challenge matches and fit labels are practical rules, not individually validated predictions.
